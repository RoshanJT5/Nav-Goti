## What is Nav Goti?

**Nav Goti** (also known as Navakankari) is an ancient Indian board game that has been played for centuries in the Sindhu region and across India. The name "Nav Goti" literally means "Nine Pieces" in several Indian languages:
- **Nav** (नव) = Nine
- **Goti** (गोटी) = Piece/Token

This game is known by various names across India:
- **Navakankari** (Karnataka)
- **Char Bhar** (Punjab)
- **Sholo Guti** (Bangladesh - 16 pieces variant)

The game represents India's rich tradition of strategic board games and has been passed down through generations as a test of tactical thinking and planning.

## Cultural Significance

### Historical Context
Nav Goti has been played in India for centuries, with archaeological evidence suggesting similar games existed in ancient civilizations along the Indus Valley. The game demonstrates:

1. **Strategic Thinking** - Planning multiple moves ahead
2. **Pattern Recognition** - Identifying mill formations
3. **Resource Management** - Managing limited pieces
4. **Tactical Adaptation** - Adjusting strategy based on opponent moves

### Regional Variations
While the core rules remain consistent, Nav Goti has regional variations:
- **Number of pieces**: 9 (standard), 12, or 16
- **Board layouts**: Triple square, cross pattern, or star pattern
- **Flying rules**: When reduced to 3 pieces in some variants
